PDFHarbor Privacy Policy

Effective 6 August 2026 · Last updated 6 August 2026

PDFHarbor does not collect, transmit, or sell your data. It has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, and no servers.

Every feature — viewing, merging, splitting, compressing, scanning, converting, watermarking, signing, and password protection — runs entirely on your device. Your documents never leave it unless you explicitly share or print them yourself.

The released app does not even request the Android INTERNET permission, so it cannot send your files anywhere.

Who we are

This policy covers the Android application PDFHarbor (package com.pdfverse.app), referred to below as “the app”. The app is published by Sachin Maharjan (“we”, “us”).

If you do not agree with this policy, please stop using the app and uninstall it.

Information we collect

We collect nothing. We do not operate any server that the app talks to, and the app contains no analytics, crash-reporting, advertising, or tracking SDK. We have no way to see your files, your file names, your usage, or your device identifiers.

Specifically, the app does not:

  • require you to create an account or provide an email address, phone number, or any other personal detail;
  • upload your PDFs, scans, photos, or signatures to any cloud service;
  • record analytics, telemetry, usage statistics, or advertising identifiers;
  • display ads or share anything with advertising networks;
  • sell or otherwise disclose any personal information to third parties.

Information stored on your device

To work as a document app, PDFHarbor keeps some information locally, in the app's own private storage on your device. It is never transmitted, and it is deleted when you uninstall the app or clear its data.

What is storedWhy
Your preferences (theme, default page view, default compression level, language) So the app opens the way you left it.
A list of recently opened files (file path and the time you opened it) and files you marked as favourite To populate the Recent Files and Favourites lists on the Home and Files screens.
Bookmarks you create while reading (document and page number) So you can jump back to a page you marked.
Signatures you draw or import, saved as image files in the app's private storage So you can reuse a signature without redrawing it. You can delete any saved signature from the Signature screen.

Files the app creates

When a tool produces a result — a merged PDF, split pages, a compressed copy, exported images, a scan — the app writes a new file to the public Documents/PDFverse folder on your device (or, if that folder is not writable, to the app's private storage). Your original files are never modified in place. These output files belong to you: they stay on your device, and deleting them is entirely up to you.

Permissions and why the app asks for them

Android will ask for these permissions only when a feature needs them. You can decline any of them; the affected feature simply won't work, and the rest of the app continues to function.

PermissionUsed for
Camera Capturing pages in the document scanner. Images are processed on your device and are not uploaded. The app does not record video or audio.
Photos and media (READ_MEDIA_IMAGES, READ_MEDIA_VIDEO, and legacy storage read/write on older Android versions) Letting you pick images for the Images → PDF, watermark, and signature-import features, and saving results to your Documents folder.
All files access (MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE) The built-in file manager lists PDFs stored anywhere on your device, not only files this app created. The app reads PDF files to show them to you; it does not scan, catalogue, index, or transmit anything, and it never sends a file listing off the device. If you decline, you can still open individual files through the system file picker.
Notifications Declared in the app's manifest but currently unused — this version sends no notifications of any kind, promotional or otherwise.

When information leaves your device

Only when you choose to send it. Two actions can do this, and both are started by you:

  • Share. Tapping Share hands the selected file to the Android share sheet so you can send it via another app (email, chat, cloud storage, and so on). Once the file reaches that app, the receiving app's own privacy policy applies — we have no visibility or control over it.
  • Print. Printing passes the document to Android's print service and the printer or print provider you select, which are governed by their own policies.

The app also opens external links (the Play Store listing and this policy) in your browser. Opening a link hands control to that app; we receive nothing from it.

Third-party components

The app is built with open-source libraries that run locally on your device and do not phone home. Two components are worth naming explicitly:

  • Google ML Kit Document Scanner, part of Google Play services, powers camera capture, edge detection, and perspective correction in the Scan feature. The scan itself is processed on your device. Google Play services is a Google product governed by Google's Privacy Policy, and Google may download scanner components to your device and collect its own service data under that policy.
  • Google Play. If you installed the app from Google Play, Google collects install and, where you have opted in, crash and performance data under its own policy. We do not receive your identity from these reports.

Children's privacy

The app is a general-purpose document utility and is not directed at children under 13. Because it collects no personal information from anyone, it collects none from children.

Security

Your documents stay on your device, so their security rests on your device's own protections — screen lock, disk encryption, and the Android permission model. Data the app saves for itself (preferences, recent files, favourites, bookmarks, signatures) lives in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read on a non-rooted device.

The password-protection feature encrypts a PDF with AES-256 using a password you choose. That password is never stored, transmitted, or recoverable — not by the app and not by us. If you forget it, the encrypted file cannot be opened by anyone, including us.

Your rights and choices

Because we hold no data about you, there is nothing for us to export, correct, or delete on your behalf. You remain in full control of everything the app stores locally:

  • Delete individual saved signatures from the Signature screen, or files from the Files screen.
  • Revoke any permission at any time in Android Settings → Apps → PDFHarbor → Permissions.
  • Clear all locally stored preferences and lists via Android Settings → Apps → PDFHarbor → Storage → Clear data.
  • Uninstall the app, which removes everything it stored privately. Files you deliberately saved to your Documents folder are yours and are left in place; delete them with any file manager if you wish.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of the app changes what it does with your information — for example by adding cloud sync, crash reporting, or any other network feature — we will update this policy and change the "Last updated" date above before that version is released. Material changes will also be noted in the app's Play Store release notes. Continuing to use the app after an update means you accept the revised policy.
